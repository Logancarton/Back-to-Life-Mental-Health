Depression

Depression is more than feeling sad. It can change energy, motivation, sleep, appetite, concentration, interest, self-worth, and the ability to keep up with everyday responsibilities.

Common signs

What it can look like

  • Persistent low mood, emptiness, or hopelessness
  • Loss of interest or pleasure
  • Low energy or slowed functioning
  • Sleep or appetite changes
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Feelings of guilt, worthlessness, or recurrent thoughts about death
Evaluation

Understanding the whole picture

Evaluation considers the timeline of symptoms, previous episodes, bipolar-spectrum symptoms, anxiety, trauma, grief, sleep, substance use, medical conditions, hormonal factors, current medications, and the degree of functional change.

Symptoms can overlap with other mental health conditions, sleep problems, medical conditions, substance use, medications, and major life stressors. Evaluation is used to sort through those possibilities rather than assuming one symptom equals one diagnosis.

Treatment

Care is individualized

Medication options

Antidepressants and other medications may be considered when clinically appropriate. Choice depends on the full symptom pattern, safety, previous response, family response, medical factors, interactions, and patient preference.

Therapy

CBT, behavioral activation, interpersonal therapy, trauma-focused work, and other approaches may help address thinking patterns, relationships, avoidance, loss, and behavioral withdrawal.

Recovery supports

Regular sleep, movement, nutrition, social connection, structure, and breaking tasks into manageable steps can support recovery alongside formal treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Questions patients often ask

How long does depression treatment take?

It varies. Some symptoms can improve before others, and medication effects often build over weeks. Follow-up visits help determine whether the current plan is moving in the right direction.

Can depression be part of bipolar disorder?

Yes. Depressive episodes can occur in bipolar disorders. Screening for periods of elevated or unusually irritable mood, decreased need for sleep, impulsivity, and other activation symptoms is important before choosing treatment.

What if I am having thoughts of suicide?

Thoughts about death or suicide deserve immediate attention. If you may act on them, cannot stay safe, or are in immediate danger, use emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.
