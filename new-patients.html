How it works

From scheduling
to a plan.

Four practical steps. You do not need to solve or summarize everything before you begin.

  1. 01

    Schedule

    Choose an appointment online, or call with questions about fit, availability, location, or insurance.

  2. 02

    Prepare

    Complete the requested forms and gather the medication, history, and insurance details you already have.

  3. 03

    Meet

    Use the first visit to explain what has been happening, how it affects daily life, and what you hope will change.

  4. 04

    Plan

    Review reasonable options together and decide what fits clinically, practically, and personally.

Your first visit

You bring the story.
I’ll help organize it.

The evaluation looks at more than a symptom checklist. We may consider the areas below as they are relevant—not force every detail into a single visit.

You do not need to arrive with a perfectly organized mental-health history.
  • Current symptoms01
  • Daily functioning02
  • Psychiatric + medication history03
  • Medical factors + sleep04
  • Stressors + previous treatment05
  • Your questions + goals06
Helpful, not homework

What to have ready.

Bring what is available. Missing an old record or forgetting a date does not mean you cannot start the conversation.

  • Current medications and supplements
  • Previous psychiatric medications and what happened
  • Preferred pharmacy
  • Relevant recent labs or records, when available
  • Insurance information
  • Questions you want addressed
After the evaluation

The plan should make sense to you.

Treatment is not a preset package. The next step depends on the evaluation, your goals, safety, and what is appropriate for you.

01

Options, not assumptions

Medication may be considered when appropriate. Therapy or other services may also be recommended when useful.

02

Clear tradeoffs

Benefits, risks, alternatives, monitoring, and practical tradeoffs should be discussed before treatment decisions are made.

03

Follow-up that learns

Follow-up reviews response, side effects, functioning, and new information so the plan can adapt.

Questions are encouraged. If the reasoning is not clear, ask.
